IMF - Global Financial Stability Report
Información Pública Periodicidad: semestral Última actualización: 13 Oct 2026 Fuente: www.imf.org
Executive Summary
  • ▶Resilient markets, elevated risks: strong corporate earnings, solid growth and heavy AI investment have offset geopolitical and policy drags. Financial conditions remain accommodative, but three salient risks could trigger a sharp tightening.
  • ▶AI correction risk is building on leverage: leverage is rising across the AI ecosystem as hyperscalers turn to debt financing, and the share of single stocks in leveraged ETFs has climbed to 28% from 16%.
  • ▶Sovereign bond risks have worsened: higher expected short rates and term premiums are pushing yields up, while high public debt and rollover risks remain unresolved, raising the odds of a disorderly rise in yields.
  • ▶Buffers are thinner in key places: banks with 22% of global banking assets would be weak in the 2026 stress test (18% in 2025), weaker EMs have drawn down reserves, and pension funds and insurers have nearly doubled their share of illiquid investments.
Key Change (vs. GFSR April 2026)
  • ▶From war shock to resilience with rising risks: markets weathered the war and financial conditions remain accommodative, but the IMF now sees the risks of a sharp rise in sovereign bond yields and of cyber threats as worse than in April, while the risk of an AI-driven correction is about the same.
Risk Balance
  • ▶ Sharp rise in sovereign bond yields on resurgent inflation or fiscal shocks — trend: worse.
  • ▶ Cyber threats to critical institutions and market infrastructure, with frontier AI used in attacks — trend: worse.
  • ▶ AI-driven market correction if investment or profitability disappoints, amplified by leverage and circular financing — trend: about the same.
  • ▶ Household debt burdens have eased and net worth has increased — trend: better.
Key Quote
"Financial markets have been resilient since the April 2026 Global Financial Stability Report, but financial stability risks are elevated." — Executive Summary, GFSR October 2026

Executive Summary
  • ▶Stability risks are elevated: the war in the Middle East, renewed inflation pressures and the risk of further tightening come on top of channels that could turn market turmoil into instability. Since February, global equities have fallen 8% and bond yields have risen sharply.
  • ▶Sovereign bonds are more fragile: record debt, shorter issuance maturities and a more price-sensitive investor base raise rollover risk. On auction days the US 10-year yield now moves 11 bp at the 90th percentile, versus 6 bp in 2020–22.
  • ▶Leveraged nonbanks could amplify a sell-off: hedge funds' gross fixed-income exposure exceeds $18 trillion (under $9 trillion in 2020), and equity options trade at near cash-market scale, with 0DTE options around 60% of S&P 500 options volume.
  • ▶Emerging markets are most exposed: capital flows are K-shaped and skewed toward carry-driven debt rather than FDI or equity, and nonresident nonbank investors are more sensitive to global risk than residents.
Key Change (vs. GFSR October 2025)
  • ▶From complacent calm to a war shock: financial conditions have tightened since October 2025, by less than in past global shocks, and remain accommodative by historical standards. The baseline Growth-at-Risk for 2026 improves to 0,75% from 0,5%.
Risk Balance
  • ▶ Prolonged war: conditions tighten about 1,1 SD and Growth-at-Risk falls to –0,5% in the downside scenario.
  • ▶ Forced selling by hedge funds, option sellers and leveraged ETFs amplifies volatility in bonds and equities.
  • ▶ Private credit defaults spread into broader corporate credit; a slowdown in AI investment hits circularly financed firms.
  • ▶ De-escalation and normalizing energy prices would lift Growth-at-Risk to 2,6% in the upside scenario.
Key Quote
"The contained market impact of geopolitical shocks do not imply that vulnerabilities are benign. Rather, it may indicate that markets have not fully priced more adverse scenarios." — Tobias Adrian, IMF Financial Counsellor · Foreword, GFSR April 2026

Global growth distribution, 2026
5th-percentile growth: 0,75% in the baseline, 2,6% if the war de-escalates, –0,5% if it drags on — a 1,2pp swing driven by the left tail, not the mode.
IMF Financial Conditions Index, by region
Financial conditions have remained accommodative, on net, across regions; non-China emerging markets are the notable exception, where conditions have tightened this year.
Emerging market composite risk index
EM risk premiums are back to compressed levels across asset classes after a brief widening at the onset of the war.
EM currencies: what drove the rally, and the reversal
The 2025 rally in EM currencies was shaped mainly by the dollar factor rather than fundamentals; since the war, earlier outperformers have seen the largest sell-offs.
10-year sovereign bond yields: change decomposition
Increases in longer-term yields have been driven by both expected short rates and term premiums, especially in advanced economies.
US equity holdings across jurisdictions
Many jurisdictions have increased their exposures to US equities, a sign of AI chasing that would transmit an AI correction abroad.
Hedge fund repo borrowing and sovereign bond exposure
Hedge funds’ repo borrowing has increased along with government bond exposures; basis trades have partly unwound without notable pressure in repo markets.
Growth-at-Risk Forecasts
Each pair of editions assesses the same target year: October gives the first reading, April revises it. Risks for 2026 have eased slightly, from 0,5% to 0,75%. The October 2026 GFSR did not update Growth-at-Risk, so 0,75% (Apr '26) remains the latest reading.
Growth-at-Risk, historical rank
Baseline sits near the 35th percentile: only moderately elevated by historical standards. The downside scenario drops it into the worst quintile.
